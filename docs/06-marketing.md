# Getting traffic without ads

## The constraint

Paid acquisition, the standard growth lever for print-on-demand, is largely
closed to political merchandise:

- **TikTok** prohibits political advertising outright. There is no
  authorization path. Organic posting is fine and is your single best channel.
- **Meta** requires authorization, ID verification, and a "paid for by"
  disclaimer for ads about social issues, elections, or politics. Political
  merchandise generally falls inside that definition, enforcement is
  inconsistent, and rejected ads can put the whole ad account at risk.
- **Google** requires election-advertiser verification and restricts what
  political ads can say and target.

Verify each policy yourself before spending — they change, and they are
enforced by classifiers that do not read your intent.

Plan as though **paid is unavailable**. If some channel turns out to work, it
is upside, not the plan.

## What that means strategically

Two things follow, and they should drive every decision:

1. **The product has to be the marketing.** A shirt nobody screenshots gets no
   traffic. This is why the joke quality bar is the whole business, and why
   twenty great designs beat five hundred mediocre ones.
2. **Owned channels are everything.** Email and SMS are the only audience
   nobody can revoke. Build the list from day one, before there is anything to
   sell.

## Channels, in priority order

**1. TikTok and Reels, organic.** Post daily. Formats that work: the design
reveal, the "things that shouldn't be controversial" list, packing an order,
the joke explained badly. Political humor travels further than product content
— lead with the joke, show the shirt second.

**2. Email list.** Popup on the store from day one. Offer 10% off. A 500-person
list that opted in because they liked the joke converts better than any
acquisition channel you could buy.

**3. Reddit, carefully.** Highly relevant and highly allergic to self-promotion.
Read each subreddit's self-promo rules, participate for real, and only share
where it's explicitly permitted. Getting banned costs more than a post gains.

**4. Creator seeding.** Free shirts to accounts with 10k–100k followers in the
niche. Cheaper than ads and it doubles as your content pipeline. Twenty shirts
is roughly $300 all-in and will outperform $300 of ads you can't legally run.

**5. Bluesky and X.** Low effort, occasional outsized hit. Good for testing
which jokes land before committing artwork.

**6. Etsy as a discovery channel.** Real search intent for political shirts.
High fees and you don't own the customer, but it's traffic you don't have to
manufacture. Worth revisiting once the Shopify store is live.

## The test-before-you-print loop

The cheapest thing you can do, and most people skip it:

1. Post the joke as plain text or a rough mockup on TikTok or X.
2. Watch engagement for 48 hours.
3. Only make artwork for the jokes that got a reaction.

This inverts the usual order — most sellers make 50 designs and hope. You are
letting the audience pick, before you spend a minute in a design tool. It also
builds the audience that will buy the winners.

## Cadence that fits the season

- **Daily:** one organic short-form post.
- **Weekly:** one drop. A recurring drop is a reason to open the email.
- **Reactive:** when news breaks, the joke ships within 48 hours. This is the
  one thing a small operation can do that a big one cannot, and it is worth
  more than any other advantage you have.

## Numbers to hold yourself to

At roughly 2% conversion and ~$11 gross profit per shirt, 100 orders needs
about 5,000 store sessions. That is a realistic organic number over five weeks
**if** a couple of posts break out — and an unrealistic one if none do. Treat
the first breakout as the signal to pour effort into whatever produced it.
