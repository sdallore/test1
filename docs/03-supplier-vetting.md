# Picking a DTF drop-ship supplier

## Understand the market first

"DTF drop-shipper" covers two different businesses, and mixing them up will
cost you weeks.

**Integrated print-on-demand** (Printful, Printify, Gooten, SPOD, Apliiq).
Native Shopify apps. An order lands, they print and ship it, you never touch
it. Highest per-unit cost, near-zero operational work, and the order routing is
already built. Most use DTG or a DTF/film hybrid rather than pure DTF.

**Transfer suppliers and contract printers** (the smaller DTF shops, including
the ones selling gang sheets). Lower per-unit cost and often better hand-feel,
but many sell *transfers* rather than fulfillment — they ship film to you, and
you press and mail it yourself. That is not drop-shipping. The ones that do
fulfill often lack a Shopify app, meaning a CSV or API integration you build
and maintain.

**The recommendation for a five-week launch: start integrated.** The margin
difference is real but it is second-order compared to being live before the
election. Move to a contract DTF printer once a design proves out and volume
justifies the integration work.

## Vetting questions — ask before you commit

Send these verbatim. A supplier who won't answer in writing is the answer.

**Content policy — ask this first, it disqualifies fastest**

1. Do you have a content policy restricting political or controversial designs?
   Send it in writing.
2. Has a political design ever been rejected after a store was live? What is the
   notice process?
3. Who decides — automated review or a human?

**Economics**

4. Per-unit cost: blank + print + fulfillment, for a front-only print on your
   standard unisex tee, at 1, 50, and 250 units/month.
5. Domestic shipping cost and method. Is tracking included?
6. Any setup, monthly, or per-design fee?
7. Volume discount thresholds.

**Operations**

8. Production turnaround in business days, measured from order receipt to
   carrier handoff — not to label creation. The distinction matters in October.
9. Current turnaround *right now*, during peak season, versus your quoted
   average.
10. Cutoff date for delivery by November 3 to a US address.
11. Defect and reprint policy. Who pays for a misprint? For a lost parcel?
12. Can you white-label? Does the packing slip carry my brand or yours?

**Integration**

13. Native Shopify app, API, or CSV? Get the docs link.
14. Do tracking numbers sync back to Shopify automatically?
15. What happens to an order if an item is out of stock mid-season?

**Quality**

16. Send a sample pack of 3 shirts with my artwork before I commit. What does
    it cost and how fast?

## The sample test — do not skip this

Order your own top design from your two finalists, on the same blank, and
compare:

- **Hand feel.** A thick plastic-feeling print is a returned shirt.
- **Wash test.** Ten cold washes, inside out, tumble dry low. Cracking,
  peeling, or fading at ten washes means find another supplier.
- **Edge quality.** Look hard at fine text and thin strokes. DTF fails first at
  the edges.
- **Color accuracy** against your file, especially any bright or neon.
- **Packaging.** Is it something a customer would photograph?
- **Actual delivery time**, measured against what they quoted you.

Budget $60–100 and a week for this. It is the cheapest insurance you will buy.

## Blanks

Ask which blanks they stock. Broadly:

- **Bella+Canvas 3001** — soft, retail fit, the default for a brand that wants
  to be worn. Costs more.
- **Next Level 3600** — similar positioning, sometimes cheaper.
- **Gildan 64000 / 5000** — cheapest, boxier, heavier. Fine for a low price
  point, wrong for a premium one.

Pick one and standardize. Every size chart, mockup, and return policy you write
is tied to that specific blank.

## Red flags

- Won't put the content policy in writing
- No sample program, or samples take more than a week
- Quoted turnaround has no "peak season" caveat in September
- No tracking sync back to the store
- Prices only visible after you've integrated
- Reviews mentioning silent order cancellations
