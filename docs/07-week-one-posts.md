# Week one — seven posts

Phase 0 of [the launch plan](05-launch-plan.md), written out. Cost: $0. The
job this week is not sales. It is finding out whether a stranger laughs.

## The kit

`python3 src/social_export.py` builds everything this week needs into
`build/posts/`: a 1080x1920 card per design, sized clear of the furniture each
platform draws over your art, plus `captions.txt` with every caption ready to
paste. The schedule itself lives in
[`catalog/week-one.csv`](../catalog/week-one.csv) — edit a caption there and
re-run rather than retyping it into an app.

Posting stays manual. Both platforms forbid automated posting outside their own
APIs, and the account most likely to get caught is a brand-new one with no
history. To space the week out, use the schedulers they give you: TikTok's
desktop uploader has a **Schedule video** toggle good for 10 days ahead, and
Instagram schedules Reels and feed posts through **Meta Business Suite >
Planner**. Both are first-party and neither risks the account.

## Rules for the week

**Nothing is for sale, so don't pretend otherwise.** No link in bio, no "shop
now", no price. A store you don't have that people can't find is the fastest
way to look like every other drop-ship account. Not having one is an advantage
this week: *"tell me which one to print"* is a better call to action than
*"buy"*, and it produces the exact signal Phase 0 is trying to collect.

**Bio:**

> Department of Decency — shirts for people who show up.
> Nothing printed yet. Tell me which one to make.

**One post a day, roughly the same hour.** Five designs, then two posts that
harvest what the five turned up. Don't dump them all at once; each needs its
own shot at the algorithm.

**Your first three posts are the baseline.** The launch plan says to compare
against your own normal numbers — on a brand-new account you don't have any
yet. Days 1–3 establish it. Judge days 4–7 against them, and judge the whole
week against itself.

**Don't delete a flop.** A post that dies is data, and deleting it doesn't
reset anything on the account. You need the losers to know what a winner
looks like.

**Answer every comment in the first hour.** Replies are the cheapest reach
there is, and one of them becomes Saturday's post.

**Expect uneven reach.** Political organic content gets distributed
inconsistently, and merch plus politics more so. One flat post is not a
verdict on the joke. Three flat posts might be.

## Day 1, Monday — Nobody Asks The Fire Department For A Copay

The strongest thing in the catalog, so it opens. It works because it isn't
asking you to agree with a policy — it's pointing at one you already accept.

**Format:** design reveal, 6–8 seconds. Hold on the hydrant, then pull back to
the full shirt graphic.

**On-screen, first frame:** *Things we already pay for together and nobody
riots about*

**Caption:** Item one: the fire department has never once asked for your
insurance card. New account, no store, just a question — would you wear this?

**Watch for:** the comment that names a second example (libraries, roads, the
9-1-1 operator). That reply thread is free content and it tells you the joke
has a shape people can extend.

## Day 2, Tuesday — Democracy Is A Group Project

The most legible joke you have — everyone did a group project, everyone
remembers who didn't help. The art is doing heavy lifting here, so let it.

**Format:** slow zoom on the cartoon poster board, then the line. The art is
warm and a little silly; don't undercut it with a serious voiceover.

**On-screen, first frame:** *and we all know who isn't doing their part*

**Caption:** Every group project has the person who shows up on the last day
asking what the font should be. Democracy is no different.

**Watch for:** shares over likes. This one is built to be sent to a specific
person, which is exactly the behaviour that grows an account.

## Day 3, Wednesday — I'm With The Banned

There are thousands of competing listings for this pun. That's the point of
posting it: you are not testing the joke, you are testing whether *your*
version of a joke people have already seen still travels.

**Format:** list video. Five banned titles on screen, one per beat, then the
shirt as the payoff.

**On-screen, first frame:** *Books someone tried to get pulled from a school
library this year*

**Caption:** The list is longer than the video. Shirt's at the end.

**Watch for:** whether it outperforms days 1 and 2. If the most-competed
design is also your best post, the pun isn't the asset — the account is. If it
underperforms, that's a real argument for cutting it before you print.

## Day 4, Thursday — Decency Is A Policy Position

The least funny of the five and the most likely to be quoted. Don't try to
make it a joke; it isn't one.

**Format:** plain and slow. The design on screen, no cuts, no trending sound.
Let the sentence be the whole thing.

**On-screen, first frame:** *An actual memo from the Department of Decency*

**Caption:** Not a joke, not a slogan, just the position. Cruelty is a choice
someone makes on purpose and calls realism.

**Watch for:** saves. Statement designs get saved rather than laughed at, and
saves predict a sale better than likes do.

## Day 5, Friday — Unionize Your Group Chat

Very online, narrow, and funny to the people it's for. Friday is the right day
for it.

**Format:** fake screenshot. A group chat where someone proposes collective
action about the restaurant choice, then the shirt.

**On-screen, first frame:** *we are NOT going back to that place*

**Caption:** Solidarity begins at home. Specifically in the chat where four
people are ignoring the poll.

**Watch for:** comment volume rather than reach. This one is a test, not a
bet — if it draws a small crowd who are obviously *the* crowd, that's a
success even on modest numbers.

## Day 6, Saturday — Answer the best comment

No new design. Take the single best comment from the week and make it the
whole post.

**Format:** TikTok's reply-to-comment video. The comment sits on screen, you
answer it in eight seconds.

**Why this beats a sixth design:** it shows up in the commenter's
notifications, it proves there's a person behind the account, and it costs
nothing to make. It is reliably the highest-performing post a small account
can produce.

**Pick the comment that argues with you, not the nicest one.** Disagreement
travels further, and answering it warmly is the entire brand.

## Day 7, Sunday — Which one do I print?

The close. All five, one ask.

**Format:** all five designs in sequence, four seconds each, numbered 1–5.

**On-screen, first frame:** *I can only afford to print one of these*

**Caption:** Genuinely undecided and genuinely broke. Number in the comments
and I'll make the winner.

**Watch for:** this is the post that decides Phase 1. Not the vote count — the
comments that say "I'd buy 3" or name a size. One of those is worth a hundred
votes.

## What you're looking for on Sunday night

The gate in the launch plan hasn't moved: **nothing gets printed until one
design clearly beats your own baseline and draws at least one unprompted "I'd
buy that."**

Three outcomes:

| What happened | What it means |
|---|---|
| One design obviously outran the others and people asked to buy it | Phase 1, one design, one colour |
| Everything landed flat but the same *type* of joke got the replies | The format works, the five don't. Write five more and run the week again — still $0 |
| Everything landed flat and nothing drew a reply | The account is new and one week is a small sample. Run a second week before concluding anything, then believe it |

The cheapest outcome is the second week of posting. The expensive outcome is
printing on a week that didn't earn it.
